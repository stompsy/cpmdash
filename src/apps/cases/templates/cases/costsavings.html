{# Introduction Section #}

Our community paramedic program reduces emergency system strain by connecting high-utilizers with appropriate care pathways. By comparing emergency service use before and after enrollment, we quantify real-world impact on 911 calls, ambulance transports, and ED visits.

Observed Reductions.
The metrics below represent observed reductions in emergency service utilization and their associated cost savings using conservative unit rates.
Measurable Value.
These figures demonstrate how proactive, community-based care delivery generates measurable value for both patients and the healthcare system.
{# Narrative Data Story #}
{# 1. 911 Calls #}

It starts with the call

High utilizers often rely on 911 as their primary access point for healthcare. By providing a direct line to a community paramedic, we intercept these requests before they become emergency dispatches.

{# 3. ED Visits #}

Keeping beds open

{# Cost Scope Clarification #}

Important context on cost estimates

The ED savings figure shown above now uses a blended baseline of ${{ cost_metrics.ed_encounter_unit_cost|intcomma }} per encounter (average of Harborview + UWMC + Olympic Medical Center CPT 99281-99285 gross charges). Real encounters often include added diagnostics and treatment services beyond that base.

Taxpayers and public systems

Avoided ED utilization helps reduce public payer pressure and downstream burden on county-funded systems.

Hospitals and ED throughput

Fewer preventable encounters support capacity for true emergencies and reduce unreimbursed resource utilization.

EMS, fire, and law enforcement

Reduced crisis-driven utilization means fewer repetitive high-acuity dispatches and better unit availability across the response system.
